# What gets an informal worker hired: evidence brief (16 November 2026)

Gathered by an AI research agent with web search; every finding carries its source. Check sources before citing.

## Summary

The causal evidence from South Africa, Kenya, Ethiopia and Uganda points to one mechanism above all: an informal worker gets hired when a trusted third party vouches for something the employer cannot see. A single standardised reference letter raised South African employer callbacks by about 60% and lifted women's employment 13 points; public skill certificates raised employment from 31% to 36% and earnings 34%, while private certificates that only the worker held did nothing for employment; Ugandan soft-skills certificates made managers update beliefs and improved matches; and in Nairobi, training plus a job referral cut unemployment 10 points and raised earnings 37% while training alone did nothing. On platforms the same rule holds: Biscate workers with even one client comment got 13 times more requests, Lynk customers always chose the most-rated Pro until Lynk's own vetting and fixed pricing carried the trust and gave new Pros work within hours, and SweepSouth hands new cleaners ten free five-star ratings to break the cold start. Search subsidies (bus fare) help quickly but fade; teaching people to present themselves has lasting effects; job fairs produce almost no hires but correct beliefs; small application costs screen out exactly the women and inexperienced workers who turn out to be good. Meanwhile actual hiring runs through networks: over half of SA job seekers enquire at workplaces and 45% use friends and relatives, households take domestic workers on a former worker's say-so, day labourers are picked by passers-by at 1,000 roadside sites, and jua kali trades are entered by apprenticeship. Employers reject informal candidates because CV-led screening favours formal titles, they fear the cost of a bad hire, and they demand gating documents (ID, police clearance, a guarantor, a bike or smartphone). Referral networks also favour men. The design implication for Yoma is to manufacture credible, employer-visible vouching (references, verified ratings, simple skill checks) and push it into the channels where small employers already hire.

## Findings

- Attaching a standardised reference letter from a past employer to a South African job application raised employer callbacks by 61% (from 4.15% to about 6.7%); encouraging young job seekers to obtain letters raised women's employment by 13 percentage points (about 25%) after three months, with no effect for men; cash incentives did not increase take-up but information about the letters' value did, because job seekers underestimate what a reference does for them (Abel, Burger and Piraino, AEJ: Applied, Gauteng, n=441/1,267/498). (https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/research-paper/806_Role-of-Reference-Letters_South-Africa_June2020_2.pdf, 2020)
  Relevance: Mechanism: a third-party vouch for reliability. Informal workers have past customers who could vouch but no mechanism to collect it; women gain most; the product must also tell the worker that the reference matters, not just make it easy.
- Certifying 6,891 Johannesburg work-seekers' numeracy, communication, grit, focus and planning with standardised assessments raised employment from 31% to 36% and earnings by 34%; the effect came through hiring and higher hourly wages, and public certificates that employers could see drove it, while giving results only to the worker had no employment effect (Carranza, Garlick, Orkin and Rankin, AER). (https://www.povertyactionlab.org/node/3196342, 2022)
  Relevance: Mechanism: a checkable skill signal that reaches the employer. The signal must be visible and verifiable on the employer side; a self-held score does nothing, which is the gap between YoCard's 'stated by you' and 'confirmed' tiers.
- In Uganda, disclosing a soft-skills certificate (creativity, trustworthiness, helpfulness, attendance, communication) during recruitment at 422 SMEs led managers to raise their assessment of workers, matched higher-skilled workers to higher-ability managers and raised earnings conditional on employment; firm owners said soft skills were valuable but unobservable, whereas practical skills they could already test (Bassi and Nansamba, Economic Journal; 787 workers, 2014-2018). (https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/information-frictions-labor-market-evidence-field-experiment-uganda, 2022)
  Relevance: Small employers can test hand skills on the spot; what they cannot see is reliability and attitude, so that is the signal worth certifying for a car washer, braider or day labourer.
- Four hours of LinkedIn training inside Harambee's job-readiness programme raised end-of-programme employment by 7 percentage points (10%), persisting 12 months, and raised LinkedIn account ownership by 31 points from a 48% base; the authors attribute the gain to reduced information barriers, that is seeing on-platform job ads and other users' profiles (Wheeler, Garlick, Johnson, Shaw and Gargano; 1,638 job seekers, 30 cohorts, 2016-2019). (https://www.harambee.co.za/linkedin-to-job-opportunities-experimental-evidence-from-job-training-in-south-africa/, 2021)
  Relevance: Mechanism: putting the worker where vacancies and peers are visible. For informal workers the equivalent public space is WhatsApp and Facebook groups, not LinkedIn; a public profile in the channel employers already read is what moved hiring.
- In Addis Ababa, a bus-fare subsidy made young job seekers 25-32% more likely to be in formal employment at eight months, but the gain had dissipated by the four-year follow-up; a cheap job-application workshop that taught people to present their skills (CVs, tests, interviews) produced lasting gains in earnings, job satisfaction and job duration four years later (Abebe, Caria, Fafchamps, Falco, Franklin and Quinn; 3,000+ youth). (https://g2lm-lic.iza.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/GLMLIC-Policy-Brief_022-1.pdf, 2018)
  Relevance: Mechanism contrast: lowering search cost gets a first job faster but does not change the trajectory; making skills observable to employers does. For 'job development' the durable lever is presentation, not subsidised travel.
- Randomised job fairs in Addis Ababa matching large firms with young job seekers produced very few hires (about one hire for every 10-12 firms attending) because of mismatched expectations about wages, requirements and candidate quality; but after the fairs both workers and firms invested more in formal search and workers lowered reservation wages to realistic levels (Abebe, Caria, Fafchamps, Falco, Franklin, Quinn and Shilpi). (https://g2lm-lic.iza.org/2021/03/10/matching-frictions-and-distorted-beliefs-evidence-from-a-job-fair-experiment/, 2021)
  Relevance: Face-to-face matching events alone do not produce hires; what they fix is beliefs about pay and requirements. A tool that tells a worker what local jobs actually pay and require may do more than another matching event.
- For an Addis Ababa employer, a small cash incentive for completing the application test raised the quality of the applicant pool (cognitive scores +0.25 points, GPA +0.1 SD) at one-sixteenth the cost of doubling the wage; the gain came from women, the unemployed and less-experienced job seekers who normally face the highest application costs, and recruiters underestimated the benefit (Abebe, Caria and Ortiz-Ospina, AER; 4,689 job seekers). (https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20190586, 2021)
  Relevance: Mechanism: the cost of applying screens out good candidates who are poor. Removing transport, data and printing from the application step (apply by voice note, employer comes to the group) changes who gets hired, not just how many apply.
- In Nairobi, Sama's digital-work training combined with a job referral raised monthly earnings by 37% (from a KSh13,440 base) and cut unemployment by 10 percentage points (from 30%); training alone, without the referral, had no effect on employment or earnings; gains were larger for women (Atkin, Schoar and Wahnschafft, J-PAL/IPA evaluation). (https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/research-paper/Samasource%20Evaluation%20Final%20(2)_0.pdf, 2021)
  Relevance: Mechanism: an institution putting its name behind the candidate with a specific employer. The referral, not the skill, is what caused hiring; Yoma's 'confirmed' tier only matters if an employer receives it as a referral.
- In a Malawian firm's recruitment drive, men overwhelmingly referred other men even when asked to refer a woman and even under performance pay, and women did not refer enough qualified women to offset it; segregated networks did not explain it, so referral-based hiring systematically disadvantages qualified women (Beaman, Keleher and Magruder, Journal of Labor Economics). (https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/lab823/BKM_recruitment.pdf, 2018)
  Relevance: Word-of-mouth hiring, the dominant channel for informal work, has a built-in gender bias. A tool that makes a woman's record visible outside her own network (references, ratings) counteracts the channel's bias, consistent with women gaining most from reference letters.
- Stats SA QLFS data show the most used job-search methods in South Africa are enquiring directly at workplaces (57.2% in 2009, 52.6% in 2015) and seeking help from relatives or friends (39.5% rising to 44.9%), with answering or placing adverts at 36.9%; waiting at the street side for casual work was the other method whose use fell. (https://businesstech.co.za/news/business/140743/the-heartbreaking-way-people-are-forced-to-look-for-work-in-south-africa/, 2016)
  Relevance: Hiring for low-skill work in SA mostly happens by walking in and by personal networks, not through portals. The worker needs something to hand over or say at the gate, and something a friend can forward.
- Qualitative studies of South African domestic workers find that households take on new workers mainly through personal referrals from current or former domestic workers, which workers themselves regard as more reliable than online search; migrant Malawian and Zimbabwean male domestic workers in Johannesburg find employers through friends and family already working in SA, and recruiters now also post in Facebook groups asking who employs or knows someone who employs such a worker. (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02255189.2023.2166024, 2023)
  Relevance: Household hiring runs on a trusted worker's vouch for another worker. A 'worker-refers-worker' flow (a cleaner forwarding a friend's card to her employer) matches how households already hire; Facebook group posts are the digital version.
- About 45,000 day labourers, mostly men, wait at close to 1,000 roadside hiring sites in South Africa six to seven days a week with no guarantee of being picked; employers drive up and choose from those present, some workers display tools (building, carpentry, gardening, tiling) to signal a trade, and employer-worker interactions at Tshwane sites were found to be haphazard, sometimes manipulative and exploitative, with immigrants competing for the same casual jobs (Schenck, Xipu and Blaauw 2012; Blaauw, Botha and Schenck 2018; national survey n=3,830). (https://sajems.org/index.php/sajems/article/view/2087, 2018)
  Relevance: Roadside selection is a snap judgement on visible cues (tools, appearance) with no record of past work. Any portable proof a worker can show in seconds at the kerb (a card, a phone screen, a number to call) changes the one moment where hiring happens.
- On Mozambique's Biscate USSD platform, workers with at least one client comment on their profile received on average 13 times more service requests than workers with none (only 15% of active workers had one in February 2020); tenure in the occupation was the only characteristic that predicted more services performed, while age, gender and education did not; the platform's ranking also favoured long-registered workers over newcomers. (https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/jobs/how-app-helping-mozambiques-informal-service-workers-earn-more, 2021)
  Relevance: For household and small-business customers choosing a tradesperson, one piece of customer feedback dominates every other signal. The bottleneck is getting the first comment, not building a profile.
- On Lynk in Nairobi, customers in the open-quote model picked the Pros with the most completed jobs and best ratings, so new Pros with zero ratings could not win first jobs and some quit; quotes were slow and inconsistent and eroded trust. After the pivot to fixed-price, pre-paid services with Lynk assigning Pros automatically (SMS yes/no), customers stopped choosing individuals because they trusted Lynk's vetting, new Pros got jobs within hours of signing up, and customers could get a Pro within two hours. (https://theflip.africa/newsletter/lynk-lessons-learned-the-hard-way/, 2022)
  Relevance: Two hiring mechanisms: individual ratings (which lock out newcomers) and a brand's vetting (which lets newcomers in). A Yoma-backed 'vetted' mark with standard priced services can carry trust that a new worker cannot yet earn alone.
- SweepSouth computes a cleaner's rating as the average of her most recent 35 customer ratings, gives every new SweepStar 10 free five-star ratings at the start 'to reflect prior experience' so the score is stable before real bookings, and ties monthly rewards to completing 100% of bookings, holding five stars over 15 bookings, finishing a 400-hour probation and having no incidents over at least 10 bookings; platform workers' median monthly earnings were R5,242 against R3,404 off-platform in 2024. (https://help.sweepsouth.com/hc/en-us/articles/14894614085778-How-ratings-work-on-SweepSouth, 2024)
  Relevance: A live gig platform in SA solves the cold-start problem by seeding reputation, and defines 'hireable' as reliability (showing up, no incidents) more than skill. These are the metrics a trust card should carry if it is to be read by households and platforms.
- Harambee says CV-led recruitment is a 'blunt instrument' that favours youth with formal job titles and screens out candidates with the behaviours and potential to succeed; it recommends behavioural screeners, problem-solving assessments, candidate voice notes, custom questions and geo-matching to candidates living near the site, and its Breaking Barriers report says employers using such signals saw up to a 50% increase in hires and a 5-6 point rise in young people entering a first formal role; separately, small businesses tell Harambee they avoid hiring because it is risky, costly to get wrong, hard to specify, and cheap candidates are inexperienced first-timers. (https://www.itweb.co.za/article/hiring-for-potential-how-employers-can-find-young-talent-beyond-the-cv/mQwkoM6YJVl73r9A, 2026)
  Relevance: Why informal candidates are rejected: the screen is the CV and the fear is a bad hire. Voice notes, short behavioural checks and proximity are the signals Harambee's own employer data say convert; the 50% figure is self-reported by Harambee (https://www.harambee.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/HAR2568-Breaking-Barriers-June-2025-R1-1.pdf; small-business piece https://www.harambee.co.za/the-art-of-recruitment-for-small-businesses/, 2017).
- In Kenya a police Certificate of Good Conduct (KSh1,050, valid one year, historically a one-to-four-week wait) is demanded by many employers and was described as the standard requirement for security and hospitality jobs in the informal sector, so that lacking it is 'a ticket to the jobless corner'; the DCI launched a biometric system in 2026 promising delivery in 24 hours. Glovo Kenya likewise gates couriers on being 18+, holding a national ID, owning a bicycle, motorbike or car and a smartphone, and pays third-party fleets KSh85 per order. (https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/entertainment/news/article/2001278496/why-certificate-of-good-conduct-is-a-ticket-to-jobless-corner, 2026)
  Relevance: Hiring is often gated by a document or an asset, not by skill: ID, police clearance, a bike, a smartphone. A hiring tool must help the worker obtain or substitute these gating items (or point to employers that do not require them), see also https://thekenyatimes.com/breaking-news/certificate-of-good-conduct-to-be-delivered-in-24-hours-as-dci-unveils-new-biometric-system/ and https://biznakenya.com/?p=169538.
- Among 481 jua kali artisans in Nairobi's Eastlands, 86.3% learned their trade by apprenticeship inside the sector and only 12.7% at TVET institutions; 62% rely on referrals as their main way of reaching customers; earlier surveys found more than three-quarters of workshop enterprises had apprentices (1 to 20 per firm). A Nigerian employer guide notes that the lack of reliable national identification makes verifying domestic staff nearly impossible, so employers insist on an acceptable reference and guarantor before hiring drivers or domestic workers. (https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1417302.pdf, 2024)
  Relevance: Entry into a trade (job development from piece work to a position) happens through an apprenticeship with a known master, and in Nigeria through a guarantor; both are personal vouches that substitute for weak formal ID. A tool that formalises 'who will vouch for me' maps onto both routes (Nigeria source: https://nairametrics.com/2012/10/13/how-to-avoid-employing-the-wrong-domestic-staff, 2012).
